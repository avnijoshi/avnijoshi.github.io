Klock-It!

Home maintenance services are rushed for after a fault becomes a visible breakdown. This project asks what happens when care starts earlier, and what a proactive homecare service built on foresight, trust and convenience could look like for urban dwellers.

Survey

A primary survey of 50 urban Indians on who looks after the home, and how. Most respondents live in tier 1 cities (Bengaluru, Ahmedabad, Delhi NCR, Mumbai).

What the survey says about the need

66%

have to decide if something needs attention, find a technician, or remember when servicing is due.

44%

48%

name finding a trusted technician as the most challenging part of the service journey.

52%

look after the home through two or more different channels.

Share of all 50 survey responses.

In-depth interviews

Of the survey respondents who said they are the primary decision makers for their family, 15 were interviewed in depth.

01 · Fix it when it breaks

Care is reactive, with one or two exceptions

Most people check on things only when something has failed. The AC is the usual exception, serviced once a year before summer. One participant kept using a noisy washing machine for a year or two. The motor failed, a ₹6,000 repair that a monthly cleaning powder would have avoided.

“The older generation in India generally has a reactive mindset. They prefer paying for repairs only when something breaks rather than spending on preventive checks.”

02 · Trust

Trust is lost before the technician arrives

People worry that technicians will invent problems or upsell, and that a visit fee will buy no fix. Recommendations from people they know are what finally build trust, which is why a “known technician” is the first choice.

“You have to find a carpenter, he will give a quote, you don’t know whether that’s an appropriate quote or not. So trust is not there.”

03 · Slipping through

Things get forgotten when life moves

A water purifier was serviced while the warranty lasted. Then the warranty ended, the owner moved cities, and nobody remembered it. The problem only showed up on a visit home. She was also the person her parents relied on for anything that needed an app.

“The warranty also ended, and I also came here, so I forgot about it.”

04 · Scattered cover

Warranties and AMCs do not earn belief

Some households skip extended warranties altogether after seeing one fail. Others do buy them, and then juggle a separate contract and contact for every appliance, with no single place to see what is covered or due.

“They took a 3-year extended warranty, and immediately like a month or two after the extension period, the TV was out.”

Early signals for a preventive service

  • Preventive care already exists where the stakes are high, such as ACs, air purifiers, water filters and cars. Low-ticket items like toasters are simply replaced. A domain expert advised narrowing the scope into buckets rather than covering everything.
  • One pair of participants rated the idea of periodic preventive checks 7 out of 10 for usefulness.
  • Another participant said she would use it, and named routine plumbing pressure checks, to clear fluoride build-up every 6 to 7 months, as the most useful part.

Persona

The Proactive Planner

Already doing it, by hand

Organised and motivated, with AMCs and warranties already in place. The effort is the barrier, not the will. Care is tracked across notebooks, WhatsApp reminders and vendor apps, and a missed technician or a lapsed warranty has burned them before.

Needs
One provider instead of many, and proof that the money already spent is working.
Trust means
Reliability and transparency: visible service logs, technician credentials, before-and-after records.

The Delayer

Selectively preventive

Proactive for one or two things, such as the AC, and reactive for the rest. Price sensitive, and acts only when something forces the issue. Often feels they are better off not knowing about a problem, and doubts providers while still wanting certainty.

Needs
An outside prompt, and someone else to own the remembering and scheduling.
Responds to
A free or bundled first check, far more than a paid plan sold on value.

Needs Triangulation

No early warningWear builds silently. Nothing tells a household what an appliance needs until it stops.
Effort outweighs urgencyFinding, booking and waiting at home costs more than ignoring the problem, so care slips.
Unreliable processQuotes, parts and fixes are taken on faith, with little proof the work was done or will last.
Underlying need
Foresight+Convenience+Trust
How we respond
Care before failureWear builds silently. Nothing tells a household what an appliance needs until it stops.
Easier than ignoringBooking, slots and home access sorted in a few taps, including for a caretaker managing from afar.

Introducing Klock-It!

The Product, Step by Step

Step by step
01Discover

It starts with a neighbour and a free audit

The first screen offers a free home audit. People reach it through neighbours who already use Klock-It!, or through a promoter at the society who helps set up the app.

02Set up home

Describe the home once

Add the appliances with their brand and age, then the conditions in your area. This is entered once and every later booking starts from it.

03Home

Fix it now, or plan ahead

The home screen has two tabs, laid out like a food-delivery app. Instant is for the problem you have today. Longevity is for care on a plan.

04Klockit Instant

See the price before booking

Pick the appliance and the issue, then a 2-hour arrival slot. The rate card shows the price upfront, before any payment.

05Booked

Care has been klocked in

The booking is confirmed in the app and on WhatsApp, so the household knows the visit is held.

06My bookings

Know who is coming

The expert’s name, Klockit ID and a visit code appear before the visit, and every step of it is tracked live.

07The visit

Proof of every visit

The expert measures before and after the repair. The household gets both readings and a standard bill.

08Klockit Longevity

A plan built from the readings

After the audit, a plan is suggested for the home. The household chooses what goes on it.

09Sustain

The whole home at a glance

One screen shows this visit, the money saved and the next visit.

10Appliance dashboard

How each appliance is doing

Every appliance carries a health score, its last service and its next check, all taken from real readings.

Revamping Brick&Bolt’s Referral Web Experience

Referred customers convert at more than twice the company average, but the referral page brought in about three leads a month. I audited the page, studied how other programmes win referrals, and redesigned it to explain the offer, build trust and get people to the form.

Role
Product Designer
Team
Product, Brick & Bolt
Duration
3 weeks, March 2025

Project brief

Referred leads convert well. But, only few of them arrive.

Referral and offline leads together made up 17% of company GMV in December 2024, and the 2025 plan was to grow them to about three times that size.

of company GMV came from referral and offline leads in December 2024.

28.5–40%

meeting-to-conversion for customer referrals, Oct to Dec 2024, against a company average of 12%.

~3 leads

a month from about 90 visitors a month on the referral page.

Audit

What was wrong with the live page?

UX Audit: 10 Usability Heuristics

To Fix

Ambiguous banner

The headline isn’t catchy, never says “referral” and has no call to action. 7/10 employees assumed the people in the image as spouses, rather than friends who would refer each other.

Heuristic: Recognition Rather Than Recall
Aim: To reduce the cognitive load on the user

To Fix

No introduction to the programme

When asked a few non-product team employees what they inferred from ‘referrals’, many people related it to ‘job-referrals’. Customers from other walks of life can misread it too.

Heuristic: Match Between System and Real World
Aim: The design should speak the users’ language.

To Keep

Add multiple references

It saves time and lets one submission bring in several leads. Referrers include brokers and professionals who know many prospects.

Heuristic: Flexibility and Efficiency of Use
Aim: To provide accelerators by tailoring content and functionality for individual users.

To Keep

Footer link

Footer link helps the user to discover or navigate back to the Referral page.

Heuristic: Flexibility and Efficiency of Use
Aim: To provide accelerators by tailoring content and functionality for individual users.

To Fix

Referral policy page

Important terms live on a separate Referral Policy page. They belong in the FAQs, where people already look for answers.

Heuristic: Help and Documentation
Aim: Provide ‘reactive help’ to answer questions, troubleshoot user problems, or provide detailed documentation and materials.

To Fix

Cluttered steps

The steps to become a referral partner are packed together with tight line spacing, and they don’t match the updated process in the PRD.

Heuristic: Aesthetic and Minimalist Design
Aim: To reduce the ‘noise’ in the design

To Fix

Unstructured form

Nothing separates the referrer’s details from the referee’s. Fields don’t line up, white space is uneven and the text under Submit is cramped.

Heuristic: Match Between System and Real World
Aim: To make information appear in a natural & logical order.

To Fix

Missing FAQs

The process of referring has multiple stages and longer timeline depending on multiple stakeholders. The visitors are bound to have doubts which can be possible resolved in the website by listing the FAQs.

Heuristic: Help and Documentation
Aim: Provide ‘reactive help’ to answer questions, troubleshoot user problems, or provide detailed documentation and materials.

To Keep

Two channels to chat

Users can either chat with the website chatbot, or through WhatsApp bot.

Heuristic: Flexibility and Efficiency of Use
Aim: To provide accelerators by tailoring content and functionality for individual users.

To Fix

Hovering CTA

The floating CTA often blocks the text from the users.

Heuristic: Aesthetic and Minimalist Design
Aim: Don’t let unnecessary elements distract users from the information they really want.

03Insights

Institutional support

Digital support follows institutional backing, as linguistic diversity is a management responsibility.

  • Multilingualism is often seen as a resource burden, even though diversity is society’s natural state
  • Languages used officially (government, law, health, education) are far more likely to be digitally supported
  • In Sub-Saharan Africa, English signals opportunity while Luganda is tied to music and religion, so people go online in the colonial language

Language community

A language’s usage at home is the base that its collective digital use depends on.

  • Passing a language from one generation to the next is a key sign of its vitality.
  • Fishman’s revival model says to fix the early stages first. Wales applies this in Cymraeg 2050, a plan with six themes

Cymraeg 2050 is the Welsh Government’s long-term strategy to revive and grow the language, with a target of reaching a million Welsh speakers by 2050.

Language technology

Robust Language Technology can slow down ‘Language shift’ on digital mediums.

  • Apt language technology can improve Indigenous people’s quality of life
  • Missing keyboards, fonts or OS support signal real-world language vulnerability
  • Without digital language support, speakers must switch languages or be left out

Digital literacy

Access to tools without the skills and interpretable content to use them, lets the digital language divide persists.

  • Digital language divide runs from a use divide (lacking basic skills) to a quality-of-use divide (can navigate but can’t get the most out of it)
  • Text-heavy interfaces fail users with low literacy
  • Facebook in Ethiopia offers transliteration for its interface, but its community guidelines aren’t available in any Ethiopian script or language

Culture

AI can’t represent a culture it was never taught to understand.

  • A Western-controlled internet concentrates power and overlooks most of the world’s population
  • Language is tied to culture, so misrepresentation harms heritage and biased data reinforce stereotypes
  • Data gathering needs transparency and inclusivity; tech companies must scrutinize collection

Who is involved?

Unlike mass tourism, ecotourism sits between two governance systems. The Ministry of Tourism leads national strategy and promotion, the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change frames the conservation rules, and States and site-level committees carry it out.

This is what makes ecotourism tricky: its attraction is also what needs protecting, and promoting and protecting are the responsibility of different authorities.

  • Core: Definition works through them, or on them.
  • Buffer: Set the conditions the core group works in.
  • Fringes: Shape what ecotourism is, or feel its effects from afar.

Redefining Ecotourism

What if the visitor were part of the ecosystem, not just a guest in it?

Ecotourism rests on three pillars: conserve nature, sustain local people, and educate. These pillars describe what ecotourism gives. In all three, the visitor is on the receiving end.

That is the gap a fourth pillar could fill — Contribution.

It turns the visitor from a consumer of ecotourism into a contributor to the ecosystem. Regenerative tourism may still be a niche today, but it should become the norm of ecotourism.

Ecotourism Redefined

Ecotourism is responsible travel to natural areas that protects the ecology, uplifts the community, educates the visitor, and makes the visitor a contributor to the ecosystem.

Design Interventions

Introducing: Ecotourism Model Canvas

What is it

The Ecotourism Model Canvas is a planning tool for ecotourism sites. It turns the definition of ecotourism into nine questions that a site answers for itself. The questions sit in four stages:

Foundation
What makes the place special, and who is involved or affected.
Core
What measurable ecological and social commitment the site makes.
Delivery
How the right visitors arrive, what happens on site, how the visit is designed, and how capacity, waste and habitat are managed.
Outcomes
Where the money comes from and goes, and what the site will track.

Who is it for

  • Primary: People planning and running a site together, i.e; forest and range officers, tourism officers, Eco Development Committees and operators.
  • Also useful to: state tourism boards planning a new site, NGOs and consultants supporting communities, and policy makers who want to read plans side by side.
  • Not for visitors directly- they experience results but don’t fill it in.

Why it works

It answers what we saw in the field. Each insight lands on a box:

  • Skewed promotion → Environmental Assets and Key Activities
  • Weak ways to evaluate → Impact Measurement.
  • Passive guidelines & interpretation → Visitor Experience.
  • Split between tourism & conservation authorities → Stakeholders

Limitations

  • It is a planning aid, not a certification.
  • It does not set targets, since measuring ecological and economic impact was outside our scope.
  • It has not yet been tested at a site, and our fieldwork was limited to Gujarat.
